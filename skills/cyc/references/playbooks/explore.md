# Explore

Use when the desired outcome is recognizable but the approach or product premise remains unsettled. Stages: [frame](../../../../methods/cyc-frame/SKILL.md) → [understand](../../../../methods/cyc-understand/SKILL.md) → [explore](../../../../methods/cyc-explore/SKILL.md) → [challenge](../../../../methods/cyc-challenge/SKILL.md) → [deliver](../../../../methods/cyc-deliver/SKILL.md).

Frame who benefits, what improvement matters, and which constraints are firm before generating alternatives. Compare meaningfully different approaches. Give each the same criteria, expected impact, tradeoffs, uncertainty, and cheapest useful experiment. A small sketch or disposable prototype is appropriate when seeing behavior resolves a real decision. Keep prototype artifacts isolated and label their limitations; a prototype does not establish production readiness.

If every option fails the same criterion, return to the premise rather than producing more superficial variants. Record observed experiment outcomes separately from predicted advantages. Do not treat a proposed direction as user-approved, or advance into full implementation merely because one option looks promising.

**Acceptance:** a supported recommendation or a crisp decision the user must make.
