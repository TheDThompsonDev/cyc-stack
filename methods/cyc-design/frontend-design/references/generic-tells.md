# Generic tells

These are the sites a model writes when nobody tells it otherwise. Learn them so you can recognize them in your own output. There are two opposite ways to miss: a brochure, good to look at with nothing to do, and a bare page, with plenty to do and nothing to look at. Score the requested surface. Apply brochure length, visual inventory and destination checks to a full visual site. For a bounded task view, inspect the actual records, task, state and access needs recorded before implementation. Count the applicable tells present. Any brochure tell (23 to 28) fails the page, and so do tells 21, 34, 39 and 40. Otherwise three or more tells fail it, and the repair is a different decision, not a different color. One or two: fix them before extending the work.

## The brochure

These come first because styling cannot repair them. On 2026-11-21 two builders given “Build a website for a neighborhood bicycle repair shop” each produced a single page under three screens tall with seven things to act on in its first screen. One of them was using an earlier version of this method.

23. A full-site brief was reduced to one page. The navigation scrolls to sections of the same page and no link leads to another page of the site.
24. The first screen is a slogan, a sentence, one or two buttons and one picture. Fewer than twelve things to act on at desktop width.
25. The visitor is meant to compare options but is missing available records or the facts needed to choose, such as price, time, date, size or status.
26. No task a visitor can finish. Every button scrolls, opens a mail link or shows a message; nothing is searched, filtered, booked, built or checked.
27. Nothing is true now. Hours, dates and availability are written into the copy, so the page says the same thing at midnight as at noon.
28. A whole visitor-facing site ends before its promised inventory, evidence or next action. Three screens is the full-homepage benchmark. A requested single working view can fit in one screen.

## The bare page

This is what a builder produces when told only to add substance. On 2026-11-21 a barbershop built that way had six pages and a booking a visitor could finish, and its owner judged it worse than the one-page site built with no method at all. It read as a form.

34. On a visual public homepage the first desktop screen holds fewer than four pictures and none covers a quarter of it.
35. On a page whose promise depends on imagery, the first phone screen and the one after it hold no picture.
36. The choice depends on appearance or physical condition, but the entries show only text or initials. A status-first view can show comparable facts and put a useful photograph in the detail view.
37. One visual weight: every control, label and fact in the first screen is small, outlined in gray and about the same size, so nothing leads. No headline, no dominant picture and no field of color.
38. Small controls packed in to raise the count of things to act on: rows of outlined chips under 40 px tall, or a main button under 44 px.
39. The opening hides the material needed to choose. Examples include an invitation with no visible people or date, a finder with no comparable options, or a working view buried under a decorative campaign.

## The unchosen look

A page can avoid every tell above and still look like nobody decided anything. The look is five choices made from the subject and written in the decision sheet. No canvas, typeface or palette is a tell by itself; an unchosen one is, and so is the one pairing builds without this method reach for.

29. The look was not chosen: the sheet's canvas, type voice, corners or color source is missing, or its reason would fit any business.
30. The page matches the line written under “Without this method I would have built”: the same canvas, the same type voice and the same opening.
31. No signature move: nothing in the first screen that a different business's site could not also carry unchanged.
32. Nothing carries color: neither photographs, nor content, nor artwork, nor a field of the brand color. The page is black, white and gray with colored buttons.
33. Two sites built in the same request share a canvas, a type voice and a header arrangement.
40. The look builds without this method chose: a warm tint (cream or paper) behind most of the text with a serif headline, when no brand you were given sets them. Three of three such builds set the page background to cream, and two set the headline in Fraunces. Either one alone is a choice.

## The older default page

Layout

1. Centered hero: headline, one-sentence subhead, two buttons (filled and outline), everything centered, nothing else in the first viewport.
2. Three equal cards in a row, each with an icon in a tinted circle, a bold title and two lines of text. Repeated as “features”, “services” and “why us”.
3. Every section built the same way: the same max-width container, the same vertical padding, a centered heading and centered subhead, then a grid.
4. White and light gray (`#f9fafb`, `#f3f4f6`) alternating as the only rhythm.
5. A testimonials row of three quote cards with avatar circles, and a stats row of four numbers nobody measured (10k+, 99%, 24/7, 4.9 stars).
6. A footer with four columns of links and a copyright line, however small the site.

Type

7. Several type families where one would do: a display face, a text face, a label face and a fallback all visible in the first screen.
8. Display text at 36 to 48 px in a regular or bold weight with default letter-spacing and line-height 1.2, so the headline reads as large body text. A deliberately light 40 px line is a [voice](typography.md#voices), not this.
9. Interface text set large: navigation, labels and facts at 18 px or more, so few items fit and the page reads as a poster.
10. Centered text everywhere, including paragraphs.

Color and surface

11. The accent color on the button, every icon and an arbitrary headline word, so nothing says which is the action.
12. More than one brand color competing: two saturated hues given equal area.
13. Border-radius 12 to 16 px and a soft drop shadow on every box; cards inside cards.
14. A gradient blob, glass panel or mesh background behind the hero.

Imagery and copy

15. Emoji as icons, or several icon sets at different stroke widths.
16. A category illustration (a cup, a camera, a house outline) stretched to hero size, or a stock-style photo unrelated to the copy.
17. Headlines that could head any business: “Welcome to [Name]”, “Quality you can trust”, “Keep on rolling”, “Crafted with care”, “Elevate your experience”.
18. Placeholder specifics: “123 Main Street”, “John Doe”, “$99/month”, “Since 1995”, “Lorem ipsum”.
19. A pill badge above the headline reading “✨ Now live” or “New”.
20. The phone layout is the desktop layout stacked: hero text still centered, the three cards now three tall cards, and no route to the main task that stays within reach.

The opposite failure

21. Wall of text: no photograph, rendered object or color surface at least a quarter of the viewport wide anywhere within the first two viewports. See the [density rule](visual-material.md#the-density-rule). On a task-dense screen, judge whether the records and actions make the task legible.
22. Flat page: every section sits on the same canvas with the same content column; no full-bleed band, no panel, no change of surface for item-like content.

## Escaping each tell

Each row is a different decision, not a style.

| Tell | Replace with |
| --- | --- |
| 23 Incomplete site | Restore missing destinations from the agreed inventory. An expressly single view can use in-place states |
| 24 Slogan opening | The first screen of the chosen [site model](reference-standards.md): the task entry or the items themselves, real navigation and what is true now |
| 25 Nothing to choose | The records from the inventory, each with its facts in the same place |
| 26 No task | One journey built end to end with its confirmation, empty state and error state |
| 27 Nothing true now | Open or closed, the next date and availability computed from the data file |
| 28 Incomplete visit | Restore the missing evidence or next action within the agreed scope |
| 34, 35 No pictures | A first screen from the chosen model with its photograph, its items or its product in it; see [visual material](visual-material.md) for where pictures come from |
| 36 Missing visual evidence | Show equally framed examples when visitors choose by appearance, or a useful inspection photo where condition matters |
| 37 One visual weight | One thing that leads: the model's photograph, its items, or a headline in the chosen [type voice](typography.md#voices) |
| 39 Missing decision evidence | Restore the material promised in the sheet: the scene and date, comparable items, inspectable example, or records and their controls |
| 38 Small packed controls | Fewer, larger controls: main buttons 44 to 56 px tall, choices as cards or rows a thumb can hit |
| 29, 30 Look not chosen | The five choices in [Step 3](../SKILL.md#step-3-choose-the-look-from-the-subject), each with a reason only this subject gives |
| 31 No signature move | One or two moves taken from the subject and repeated down the page |
| 32 Nothing carries color | A [color source](color.md#where-the-color-comes-from) that suits the subject |
| 33 Sites that match | A different canvas or type voice for each site, chosen from its own subject |
| 40 Unguided look | A white or dark canvas under the serif, or a warm tint under another [type voice](typography.md#voices), chosen from the subject |
| 1 Centered hero | The model's first screen. For a section, a composition from the [catalog](layout-archetypes.md) |
| 2, 3 Equal cards, identical sections | Structure that follows the content: a process as a numbered list, a menu as a price list with hairline rules, work as a grid with one lead item, a comparison as a real table |
| 4 Gray and white alternation | Rhythm by density and surface: one dark or brand-colored section, one full-bleed image band, one quiet section at a narrow measure |
| 5 Invented stats and quotes | Specific proof: a real menu with prices, a named project with its brief, a plan table with actual limits. Or nothing |
| 6 Four-column footer | A footer proportional to the site: the pages, the address, the hours and a contact |
| 7 to 9 Type | One [voice](typography.md#voices), each family with one job. Interface text at 12 to 16 px. Display scale for an invitation or a compact heading above comparable facts |
| 11, 12 Color | One color for the main action and a quiet second button. See [color](color.md) |
| 13 Rounded and shadowed | One radius family; hairline borders or flat blocks; shadows only on things that float |
| 15, 16 Emoji, category clip art | One icon set at one stroke width; photographs of this business's real things, framed alike |
| 17, 18 Generic copy | Copy made of this business's nouns and numbers: the job, the price, the street, the turnaround, the date |
| 19 Pill badge | Remove it unless it states something true now, such as today's hours |
| 20 Stacked phone | The model's phone pattern: a bottom bar or pinned search, items two across or as a compact list, the action before the explanation |
| 21 Wall of text | Material from [visual material](visual-material.md): photographs, the product rendered as an object, a color surface holding content |
| 22 Flat page | One full-bleed image or color band, a tinted panel behind item-like content |

## Score sheet

Score the designed view in its ordinary presentation. A system font, simplified layout or system color supplied by the visitor's zoom, font or forced-colors settings is an adaptation to respect. A treatment required by an existing brand is recorded in the decision sheet and not counted. A passing score never offsets a barrier found on the primary path.

Record this for the first screen and for the finished site, at desktop and phone width:

```text
View: desktop 1440 | phone 390
First-screen judge: [the pass and FAIL lines it printed]
Tells present: [numbers]   Count: N   Check: fail for any of 21, 23 to 28, 34, 39, 40, or count >= 3
Changed decisions: ...
```

Counting is deliberate. It stops “it feels fine” from passing a page a reviewer recognizes as default at a glance. The count is a self-review, not a measurement of quality. A page can score zero and still be weak, which the [experience review](experience-review.md) exists to catch.
