## Applying an observation

Choose a practice that will change what you build. Record its chapter and title, the visitor’s question, the content or control you will build and what you expect to observe after using it. Reopen the entry when building or reviewing that decision. If an asset, fact or constraint changes, revise the decision in the project sheet with the reason. Keep these notes out of customer-facing copy.

- **Compose for the evidence.** A documentary scene answers “Would I belong?” Standardized photographs answer “Which fits?” A worked example answers “Can I trust the result?” A schedule answers “When can I go?” Let the strongest material determine the opening and what loses space on a phone.
- **Make the visual choices reinforce the action.** On the community page the crowd gathers around a laptop, a bold question marks building in yellow and a handwritten pointer lands on a dated event: each choice serves the same invitation. On a fit guide the comparable hemline matters more than a dramatic crop.
- **Review with the same access needs the design promises to support.** Someone who needs a step-free entrance must be able to find it, and someone comparing colors needs a written name. Follow the route on a phone and by keyboard, including the way back. Inspect the rendered crop wherever text sits on a photo.
- **Use comparisons to explain differences.** One music service set a 128 px campaign headline and 24 px player shelves. A clothing gift-guide title was 90 px and its department title 18 px. Size follows the purpose of the page.

## Banking and comparison

### The homepage and navigation

- **Put the repeat visitor’s task above the campaign.** Sign-in fields sit immediately below desktop navigation.
- **Keep the familiar entry point visible on a phone.** The phone header keeps sign-in next to the menu.
- **Put account recovery beside the field that causes the problem.** The recovery link sits beside the username and password fields, which keep their labels.
- **Explain the consequence of a convenience setting.** The remember-me explanation identifies the saved username and shared-computer risk.
- **Give trust information a quiet, consistent place.** A narrow deposit-insurance line sits above the header. The footer links to coverage details.
- **Use familiar product names for navigation.** Cards, accounts and auto loans are destinations. Search and sign-in occupy a utility area.
- **Give the hero one job.** A light panel holds one headline, one supporting line and the offer action.
- **Recompose the hero for a narrow screen.** On phone the picture leads and the copy moves onto a panel below it.
- **Give an undecided visitor a useful next step.** Illustrated goals lead undecided visitors to a specific topic.
- **Let the product demonstrate its capabilities.** A phone-interface preview shows selectable tasks such as payments.

### Type, color, shape, and spacing

- **Create a recognizable voice with one type family.** One family in weights 300, 400 and 600 carries headlines, body and labels.
- **Use scale and line length to give a light headline authority.** A narrow column gives a 40 px, weight-300 headline room for two deliberate lines.
- **Preserve body-text rhythm as display type changes.** Body stays 16 px on 24 px lines while entry headings go from 32 px on desktop to 24 px on the phone.
- **Reserve uppercase for short orientation labels.** Orientation labels use 16 px semibold type with 0.5 px spacing.
- **Give a different page purpose its own type treatment.** The help question uses 48 px at weight 100 on desktop and 40 px at 300 on phone.
- **Assign neutral surfaces distinct jobs.** White holds content, #141414 carries text, #F4F4F4 groups related items and #3D3D3D anchors the footer.
- **Give action colors predictable roles.** Blue marks links and buttons. On a product tile the filled green action checks eligibility and the outlined blue one applies.
- **Size a control for its place in the hierarchy.** The hero button is 48 px tall. The sign-in button matches its 32 px fields, keeping that form on one line.
- **Keep a common alignment through full-width sections.** Full-width sections share a 1,216 px content alignment at 1,440 px.
- **Use space to show which elements belong together.** The help question and its search field share a white band.
- **Use motion for short control feedback.** Several control transitions used 200 ms easing.

### Choosing and comparing products

- **Answer the hesitation beside the action.** The bank explains an eligibility check’s time and credit effect beside its entry point.
- **Preview a consequential process on demand.** The eligibility explanation previews requested information, available offers and acceptance.
- **Offer categories in the visitor’s vocabulary.** Travel, cash back, student use and credit building label the choices.
- **Make the current category visible.** A tint and stronger outline identify the active category.
- **Give each product the same reading order.** Artwork, credit level, fee and rewards precede the actions in each card.
- **Highlight the exact fact that is exceptional.** A limited offer highlights the qualifying benefit line.
- **Start comparison on the item someone already likes.** The comparison chooser opens with the original card already selected.
- **Keep refinement within reach on a long results page.** The desktop filter stays 16 px below the viewport top after scrolling.
- **Make filter feedback specific.** Choosing a credit level changes the phone count from 27 to 24 and enables reset.
- **Explain specialist filter terms inside the filter.** Credit-level help sits beside the levels inside the expanded filter.
- **Keep material conditions close to the claim.** Numbered references connect a benefit to that product’s terms.

### Product detail, support, and accessibility

- **Show the product in use.** A debit card at a payment terminal illustrates the account in use.
- **Offer shortcuts based on what a visitor still needs to decide.** Benefits, questions and locations have direct entry points beside account opening.
- **Explain exceptions where the promise is made.** The fee disclosure lists specific services that can incur charges.

- **Offer search and browsing together in help.** Search and illustrated topic links serve different ways of asking for help.

- **Change the layout when the reading direction changes.** Four desktop topics become a vertical phone list with an illustration beside each.

## Stay search

### Getting a search started

- **Make the main task the largest control.** Destination, dates and guests occupy the main desktop search control.
- **Distinguish a field’s purpose from its current value.** Each segment keeps its label alongside the prompt or selected value.
- **Accept uncertainty about dates.** Flexible search and tolerances of one or three days accommodate an uncertain plan.
- **Show adjacent months together on desktop.** Two consecutive months make a stay across a month boundary easier to select.
- **Make a calendar date understandable on its own.** Each day announces its full date and past dates expose why they are unavailable.
- **Explain who belongs in each count.** Adult, child and infant counters specify the age ranges.
- **Make changes visible and reversible.** The decrement is disabled at zero. Adding a guest updates the summary, enables decrement and shows a clear action.
- **Let the search header shrink when attention moves to results.** The compact results header retains destination, dates and party size for editing.

### Browsing and comparing places

- **Lead with actual inventory.** The first desktop screen shows two rows of available stays.
- **Give each row a reason to exist.** A destination or travel window gives each row a clear purpose.
- **Let visitors explore a few more items or open the whole collection.** Row arrows browse a few more places. The collection link opens the full set.
- **Give every listing a predictable reading order.** Property type and location precede price, length of stay and rating.
- **Put the unit beside the price.** The amount sits beside the number of nights. Results explain fee inclusion.
- **Reserve consistent corners for different jobs.** Recognition badges use the upper left and the save action the upper right.
- **Reveal more facts when a visitor narrows their search.** Results add dates, bedrooms, baths and review counts for shortlisting.
- **Make photographs browsable inside results.** Multiple photos with a position indicator can be browsed on a result.
- **Let geography participate in the comparison.** Map markers show prices beside the desktop list. The phone map uses the full width.

### Filters that help visitors decide

- **Offer a shortcut for common criteria.** The filter panel brings free parking and self check-in near the top.
- **Put a budget in the visitor’s own units.** A switch changes nightly prices to whole-trip amounts.
- **Show the price distribution beside the range.** A histogram shows typical prices and a text description conveys the distribution.
- **Provide a precise alternative to dragging.** Editable minimum and maximum fields accompany the slider handles.
- **Treat accessibility requirements as searchable facts.** Step-free entry, doorway width and grab bars can be filtered separately.
- **Keep a filter selection visible until the guest applies it.** A selected amenity shows a pressed state, and the panel keeps its results-count action and a way to clear.
- **Return keyboard focus to the point of departure.** Escape closes the filter dialog and puts focus back on Filters.

### Inspecting one place

- **Lead with a useful spread of photos.** One large photograph introduces the property. Adjacent views show other spaces.
- **Organize a large gallery by the question behind the click.** Shortcuts lead straight to kitchen, bedroom, balcony and bathroom photos.
- **Put capacity directly beneath the property identity.** Capacity, bedrooms, beds and baths share one line below the property identity.
- **Show the sleeping arrangement as its own section.** A bedroom photo is paired with the bed type.
- **Group a long inventory by use.** Amenities are grouped by room and use, including safety and work.
- **Give a rating a denominator.** A 4.99 score sits beside the 185 reviews behind it.
- **Let people search other guests’ experience.** Searching those 185 reviews for parking returned six.
- **Surface conditions before commitment.** Rules, safety and cancellation information have direct entry points.
- **Match the primary action to what is known.** Before dates are supplied, the main action offers availability and explains when pricing becomes exact.

### Typography, color, shape, and responsive layout

- **Use one variable sans-serif across the journey.** One variable sans serves the search controls, the listing facts and the headings.
- **Give the collection heading just enough weight to organize the row.** Destination headings are 20 px on 24 px lines at weight 600.
- **Keep comparison text compact and differentiated.** Listing titles are 13 px at weight 500; price and rating are 12 px at 400.
- **Increase the type scale when the visitor chooses a property.** The listing title is 26 px at weight 500, twice the size of a card title.
- **Let photography supply most of the color.** White and #F7F7F7 surfaces, #222222 text, #6C6C6C secondary text, #DDDDDD dividers. One pink marks the search action.
- **Concentrate personality in a small, useful detail.** Each top-level travel category pairs a miniature illustration with a plain text label.
- **Keep the photo crop and gap consistent.** A home row at 1,440 px shows seven cards about 180 px wide with 12 px gaps.
- **Use shape to distinguish controls from content.** Photos have 20 px corners, the search control is a pill and the arrow buttons are circles.
- **Use elevation where something sits above the page.** The search control casts a soft layered shadow, at most 8 px of blur at 10% black. The photo rows cast none.
- **Keep small transitions consistent.** A recurring control transition measured 250 ms with `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)`.
- **Show what a horizontal gesture will reveal.** Phone rows snap sideways through 165 px cards, with part of the next card showing at the edge.
- **Keep frequent destinations within thumb reach.** The phone’s bottom navigation groups exploring, saved places, and login, with text labels and an active-state accent.

- **Distinguish a saved fact from a finished draft.** The sample workspace separates the recorded accomplishment, the person's contribution and the next step to review it.

### Pricing and informed choices
