# What eight frontend studies taught us

Across 278 recorded practices, the closest resemblance was in **how the sites helped someone decide**. The sharpest differences were in **what deserved the most space while they decided**. A group photo and a date help someone picture attending a meetup. Six equally framed photographs help someone distinguish jean cuts. A single oversized image would serve the first job and make the second harder. That distinction reaches into art direction, typography, page structure, and behavior after a click.

## What each site gave the visitor

| Study | A question the visitor could bring | What the design put in reach |
|---|---|---|
| [Capital One](public-banking-ui-practices.md) | Can I get into my account? If I’m shopping, which card fits? | Sign-in above the campaign. Farther into the catalog, annual fees and rewards occupy consistent positions across cards. |
| [Airbnb](stay-marketplace-ui-practices.md) | Which of these places works for this trip? | Comparable photos and prices, a map showing price by location, and a room-by-room gallery once a place looks promising. |
| [Undersold](career-writing-ui-practices.md) | Will this improve my résumé and still describe work I actually did? | An inspectable before-and-after and a sample showing how a candidate’s own answer becomes a résumé line. The sample is labeled as illustrative. |
| [Dallas Software Developers](developer-community-ui-practices.md) | Can I picture myself there, and when can I go? | People gathered around a laptop, a question a member might ask, and a dated card linking to the particular meetup. |
| [Notion](workspace-product-ui-practices.md) | What would this look like doing my work? | A readable workspace preview, a reporting example with its input and expected output, and templates people can inspect before acquiring one. |
| [Target](retail-storefront-ui-practices.md) | Will this mug work for me, and how can I get it? | Capacity and material in search, care information beside the item, exact dimensions in a disclosure, and pickup alongside shipping. |
| [American Eagle](apparel-storefront-ui-practices.md) | Which cut do I like, and what size would give me that look? | Six full-length silhouettes at a comparable scale, followed by size-and-length filters and the model’s height and worn size. |
| [Spotify](music-service-ui-practices.md) | Is the subscription worth it? What do I want to hear right now? | The public page explains the trial and renewal. The player opens with actual recordings and carries a selected song into its release. |

## Where the practices converge

**Show the thing a person needs to judge.** The community photo lets people read the room. The résumé example lets them judge a rewrite before handing over a personal document. The apparel photos let them judge shape because the garments are pictured at the same scale. Each uses a different kind of evidence. A technically beautiful image can still be the wrong image if it leaves the visitor’s question unanswered. [Community](developer-community-ui-practices.md) · [Writing product](career-writing-ui-practices.md) · [Apparel](apparel-storefront-ui-practices.md)

**Put the deciding fact where doubt arises.** The bank explains what an eligibility check involves beside the action that starts it. The subscription offer puts the renewal price beside the trial. The stay listing asks for dates where the guest wants an exact price. Moving the same sentences to an FAQ would preserve the words and lose much of their value. [Banking](public-banking-ui-practices.md) · [Subscriptions](music-service-ui-practices.md) · [Stays](stay-marketplace-ui-practices.md)

**Repeat the layout where the visitor needs to compare.** A traveler needs the price and number of nights in the same place across listings. A card shopper needs the annual fee there every time. A music listener needs the track title, artist, and duration to line up across rows. Repetition is valuable *within* a set of alternatives. Applying that same repeated layout to unrelated homepages is precisely what made several CYC builds feel interchangeable. [Stays](stay-marketplace-ui-practices.md) · [Banking](public-banking-ui-practices.md) · [Music](music-service-ui-practices.md) · [CYC comparisons](../context/frontend-design.md)

**Let people see the consequence before they commit.** A bank and a clothing shop arrived at almost exactly the same mobile interaction. Choosing a credit level changed the bank’s filter button from 27 results to 24. Choosing size 4 regular changed the apparel button from 185 items to 140. In both cases the count answers “Have I narrowed this too far?” while the person can still adjust the filter. This is the kind of similarity a frontend skill should reproduce across industries. [Banking](public-banking-ui-practices.md) · [Apparel](apparel-storefront-ui-practices.md)

**Keep the visitor’s choice alive through the next interaction.** A banking comparison opens with the selected card already included. Applying a clothing size filter leaves the size visible beside the results. Opening a song from a shelf selects its track row on the release page. This is an important part of perceived quality that a homepage screenshot cannot show. [Banking](public-banking-ui-practices.md) · [Apparel](apparel-storefront-ui-practices.md) · [Player](music-service-ui-practices.md)

## Where the designs make genuinely different choices

| Design decision | One observed solution | Another observed solution | What explains the difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| What fills the opening | A community gathering and its next date | A destination/date/guest search above rows of stays | One visitor is deciding whether to participate. Another is narrowing many valid options. |
| How much space type gets | A 128 px subscription headline | 24 px desktop shelf headings inside the same music service | The campaign needs attention for one idea. The player needs room for several recognizable covers. |
| Where color comes from | Yellow connects a highlighted word, the main meetup action, and the event pointer | On the stay marketplace, listing photos supply most of the color | The community controls its visual story. A marketplace needs each host’s property to remain distinguishable. |
| What a photograph should do | An expressive group portrait provides atmosphere | Consistently framed full-length looks expose differences in hems and taper | Documentary images convey participation. Controlled crops make comparisons fair. |
| What survives on a phone | Search gains a dedicated row on the retail site | The résumé product brings the writing example forward, while the music player moves frequent destinations to the bottom | The retailer protects query editing, the writing product protects the demonstration, and the player protects repeat navigation. |
| How to handle an undecided visitor | Show a result they can inspect before uploading a résumé | Accept flexible travel dates or let someone explore templates by category | Some uncertainty calls for proof. Some calls for a control that accepts an incomplete answer. |

The clearest counterexample to a fixed brand recipe came from the workspace study. One observed homepage used Inter, blue actions, and 8 px button corners. Another used Die Grotesk, black actions, and fully rounded buttons. Both put a recognizable workspace in front of the visitor and offered separate paths for trying the product or requesting a demo. The invariant was the demonstration and the choice it supported. The study provides no evidence that either visual treatment converted better. [Workspace variants](workspace-product-ui-practices.md)

## Three observations I would carry into CYC

1. **Our current default can make different briefs converge before we pick a font.** The frontend method already asks for real content, useful actions, and deliberate art direction. It also gives a hero photograph with the task overlaid a strong default. I would let the opening follow what the visitor needs to inspect. A scene can establish belonging, a comparable set helps with browsing, a worked example demonstrates an unfamiliar claim, and a returning user can start in the working interface. For a cinema, tonight’s film times and a director retrospective could share a typeface and palette. One could open with titles and bookable times and the other with a single film still and the curator’s introduction. A short prompt should be enough for the builder to make that editorial choice. [Current frontend method](../../methods/cyc-design/frontend-design/SKILL.md) · [Earlier blind comparisons](../context/frontend-design.md)
2. **The leap in the community homepage came from several choices reinforcing one another.** The earlier design already had a real event photograph, a distinctive serif headline, and a clear button. In the preferred version, “What are you building?” gives the viewer a reason to look at the laptop in the group photo. The yellow treatment makes *building* the focal word and connects it to the action. The handwritten pointer leads to a dated meetup. Typography, crop, color, writing, and live information each contribute something the others need. That is a more useful standard for a first draft than requiring a fashionable font or an impressive hero. [Community study](developer-community-ui-practices.md) · [Design retrospective](../context/frontend-design.md)
3. **Accessibility belongs in the choice of content as well as the implementation.** A traveler can filter for a step-free entrance, a shopper can read a wash name beside a color swatch, and a listener can dismiss a track menu with Escape and resume at the same button. Those choices help people find a suitable option, identify it, and operate the interface. In first-pass review I would follow one consequential choice on a phone and with a keyboard, including what happens on return. Visual polish is a poor proxy here. In CYC’s own card-shop replay the hero passed its photo-text check while two category labels farther down became difficult to read on their phone crops. **Scope.** This synthesis covers eight selected reference studies. The 278 figure counts recorded practice bullets, including related observations across sites. The studies cover different public journeys. The general retailer’s homepage merchandising was checked against published page text because the live homepage showed a verification prompt. Findings about the music player concern signed-out use. These examples support design judgments and hypotheses for CYC. Conversion impact and whole-site accessibility remain unmeasured.
