# UI and UX practices from a public banking site

The homepage gives a returning customer a place to sign in, a first-time visitor one offer to consider, and someone still exploring a way to choose a topic. These occupy different parts of the screen. That single layout decision explains more about the clarity of the site than its palette alone.

Reviewed November 22, 2026, using public pages in Edge at 1,440 × 900 and 390 × 844, computed CSS, and the interactions identified below. Saved renders from November 21 supplied a second look at the homepage and catalog. Campaigns varied between visits. The study covers the signed-out experience through product selection and help. Accessibility observations apply to the interactions exercised. Traffic and conversion data were unavailable.

## The homepage and navigation

- **Put the repeat visitor’s task above the campaign.** Desktop visitors can enter their credentials immediately below the main navigation. Someone checking a balance can get started before reading a single offer. [Homepage](https://www.capitalone.com/)
- **Keep the familiar entry point visible on a phone.** The compact header leaves sign-in visible alongside the menu. Opening the menu reveals product categories and search. The visitor can still reach their account in one step.
- **Put account recovery beside the field that causes the problem.** The username and password fields have persistent labels. The nearby recovery link gives a blocked visitor a way forward while the problem is in front of them.
- **Explain the consequence of a convenience setting.** The remember-me control explains that it remembers the username and calls out shared computers. The explanation sits beside the choice.
- **Give trust information a quiet, consistent place.** A narrow deposit-insurance line appears above the desktop header. The footer links to coverage details for visitors who want the full explanation.
- **Use familiar product names for navigation.** Cards, accounts, auto financing, and business services are recognizable destinations. Help, locations, search, and sign-in occupy a separate utility area.
- **Give the hero one job.** One headline, a short supporting statement, and one prominent action share a light panel. The panel keeps the offer legible even when the campaign photograph has a busy background.
- **Recompose the hero for a narrow screen.** In the observed phone homepage, the image leads and the broad copy panel sits below it. The photograph keeps a recognizable subject and the headline gets enough width to wrap.
- **Give an undecided visitor a useful next step.** A row of illustrated entry points pairs each visitor goal with a brief explanation and a descriptive link. Someone can choose a direction without knowing a product name.
- **Let the product demonstrate its capabilities.** The digital-tools section pairs a phone interface with selectable tasks such as payments and credit monitoring. The visitor can connect a feature with the screen where they would use it.

## Type, color, shape, and spacing

- **Create a recognizable voice with one type family.** The sampled pages use **Optimist**, with **Helvetica Neue, Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif** as fallbacks. The homepage used weights **300, 400, and 600**. The reusable choice is the range of roles one family can cover. [Homepage](https://www.capitalone.com/)
- **Use scale and line length to give a light headline authority.** The measured desktop hero was **40 px, weight 300, with 52 px line height**. Its text occupied a **368 px** column in the observed campaign. The constrained measure made a two-line headline feel intentional.
- **Preserve body-text rhythm as display type changes.** Homepage body copy measured **16 px, weight 400, with 24 px line height**. Desktop entry headings measured **32 px** and phone equivalents **24 px**. The supporting text stayed readable as the hierarchy compressed.
- **Reserve uppercase for short orientation labels.** A measured section label used **16 px, weight 600, with 0.5 px letter spacing**. A few words identify the section before the larger mixed-case heading explains its value.
- **Give a different page purpose its own type treatment.** The help page’s opening question measured **48 px at weight 100** on desktop and **40 px at weight 300** on phone. Its search field used **24 px** type. A visitor can immediately see where to ask a question. [Help center](https://www.capitalone.com/help-center/)
- **Assign neutral surfaces distinct jobs.** White carries the main content, **#141414** carries much of the text, **#F4F4F4** groups related content, and a **#3D3D3D** footer anchors the page. Changes of surface mark section boundaries with little need for borders.
- **Give action colors predictable roles.** The homepage uses **#0276B1** blue for prominent links and buttons. On catalog tiles a filled **#25810E** green eligibility action sits above a blue outlined application action. Fill, order, and wording all reinforce the distinction. [Card catalog](https://www.capitalone.com/credit-cards/)
- **Size a control for its place in the hierarchy.** The sampled homepage hero button was **48 px high**, with **20 px** text and a **4 px** radius. Desktop sign-in used a compact **32 px** button alongside **32 px** fields with **2 px** corners. This gave the offer prominence while keeping the returning customer’s form together on one line.
- **Keep a common alignment through full-width sections.** Repeated desktop content containers measured **1,216 px** wide in a **1,440 px** viewport. Photography can reach the edges while headings, links, and controls return to a common left edge.
- **Use space to show which elements belong together.** The desktop help page gives its question and search field a white band of their own. The four illustrated topics begin together on a pale surface below. Within each topic, the heading, explanation, and link are grouped without an enclosing card. [Help center](https://www.capitalone.com/help-center/)
- **Use motion for short control feedback.** Several measured homepage transitions used **200 ms** with CSS **ease**. A small state change can register while the surrounding layout stays still.

## Choosing and comparing products

- **Answer the hesitation beside the action.** The catalog explains the estimated time and the effect of an eligibility check near its entry point. People can weigh the cost of the next step before giving personal information. [Card catalog](https://www.capitalone.com/credit-cards/)
- **Preview a consequential process on demand.** Opening the eligibility explanation showed three stages covering the information requested, the offers returned, and the later acceptance decision. Someone can inspect the process and close the dialog before starting it.
- **Offer categories in the visitor’s vocabulary.** Travel, cash back, student use, and credit building each have a labeled category control. Those labels describe reasons to choose, which helps visitors who have never heard of the products.
- **Make the current category visible.** The selected category has a tinted background and a stronger outline. Its icon and written label remain visible. On the observed phone catalog, further categories extend across a horizontal row below the introduction.
- **Give each product the same reading order.** Artwork identifies the option, a credit-level label identifies its audience, and the fee and rewards appear before the actions. Visitors can compare the same fact in the same position across tiles.
- **Highlight the exact fact that is exceptional.** A limited offer gets a small label and a highlight on the relevant benefit line. The annual fee and ordinary terms retain their usual visual weight.
- **Start comparison on the item someone already likes.** Selecting a product’s compare checkbox opened a chooser with that product already selected. Candidate alternatives showed fees and credit levels inside the chooser. The visitor keeps their starting point. [Comparison catalog](https://www.capitalone.com/credit-cards/compare/)
- **Keep refinement within reach on a long results page.** After scrolling past the desktop catalog’s filter bar, the filter control stayed **16 px** from the viewport’s top edge. Someone partway through the list can change criteria from where they are.
- **Make filter feedback specific.** On a phone-sized viewport the filter panel initially offered **27** results. Selecting excellent credit changed the action to **24** results and enabled the reset control. The visitor sees the consequence before leaving the panel.
- **Explain specialist filter terms inside the filter.** Expanding the credit-level group revealed a short help action beside the available levels. Someone unfamiliar with the classifications can investigate at the point of choice.
- **Keep material conditions close to the claim.** Numbered references sit beside qualifying benefits. Individual product tiles link to their disclosures and, where relevant, reward terms. A visitor can examine the terms of the particular option they are comparing.

## Product detail, support, and accessibility

- **Show the product in use.** The checking page opens on a debit card held at a payment terminal. Farther down, a phone notification illustrates early pay and a scannable barcode illustrates adding cash. Each image explains a different action. [Checking account](https://www.capitalone.com/bank/checking-accounts/online-checking-account/)
- **Offer shortcuts based on what a visitor still needs to decide.** The product page groups benefits, common questions, and locations into section shortcuts. The account-opening action appears alongside them for visitors who already have enough information.
- **Explain exceptions where the promise is made.** The fee question expands into concrete examples of services that can incur a charge. Readers can move from the headline promise to its practical limits on the same page.
- **Give a disclosure a keyboard-operable control and an exposed state.** In the checking FAQ, pressing Enter on the focused fee question revealed the answer and changed its expanded state from false to true. A keyboard user can perform the same reading task. [Checking FAQ](https://www.capitalone.com/bank/checking-accounts/online-checking-account/)
- **Make bypassing repeat navigation work in practice.** On the desktop homepage, the first Tab reached the skip link. Pressing Enter moved focus into an input in the page content. This saves a keyboard visitor a trip through the product menu. [Homepage](https://www.capitalone.com/)
- **Offer search and browsing together in help.** A large search field serves visitors who know what to ask. Illustrated product categories and direct links to frequent tasks give everyone else a way in. [Help center](https://www.capitalone.com/help-center/)
- **Give urgent problems a direct route.** The lost-card shortcut opened the corresponding support article. The article puts the identity-theft escalation near the top, where someone dealing with a stressful situation can find it. [Support article](https://www.capitalone.com/help-center/fraud-disputes/lost-or-stolen-debit-card/)
- **Write separate instructions when interfaces genuinely differ.** The support article has separate numbered sequences for the website and mobile app. Each uses the control names people will see on that surface.
- **State the condition for a human handoff.** The replacement instructions identify the circumstance that calls for telephone help, such as using a different delivery address. Someone can recognize the exception before repeating the digital steps.
- **Change the layout when the reading direction changes.** On desktop, four help topics sit across the page with illustrations above the text. On phone, the same topics run vertically with the illustration beside each entry. Names, descriptions, and destinations survive the change. [Help center](https://www.capitalone.com/help-center/)
