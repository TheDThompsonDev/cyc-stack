# Frontend design method

Covers: methods/cyc-design/frontend-design/**, test/frontend-first-screen.test.mjs, test/frontend-contrast.test.mjs

## Current view

The method at methods/cyc-design/frontend-design/SKILL.md is loaded by the entry skill for a new site, page, dashboard or composed redesign; a narrow repair keeps the existing identity.
It names two failures: the brochure (good to look at, nothing to do) and the bare page (plenty to do, nothing to look at). Its steps are inventory, site model and patterns, look, then build and measure.
No file under the method names a studied site. The owner asked for a skill that presents the practices without the names; the named study is docs/maintainers/frontend-reference-study.md, which scripts/pack-release.mjs keeps out of the archive.
Step 1 has the builder write pages, records, pictures, one working journey, what is true now and proof into design/decisions.md before any visual decision, from references/decision-sheet.md.
Step 2 selects a chapter and practice from the portable [frontend field guide](../../methods/cyc-design/frontend-design/references/field-guide.md). The project decision sheet connects the visitor’s question to a specific asset, record or control, phone priority and state after the click. Build and Verify reopen that decision. The [study synthesis](../maintainers/frontend-study-synthesis.md) links the full source notes. When refreshing a case, update its portable chapter too. The source-coverage test compares entries across all eight studies.
The guide opens with `Seen on several sites`: seventeen practices recorded on three or more of the eight studies, each with its count and the chapters, in descending order. A count reports the studies, not sites in general.
Chapter entries keep the studies' titles, which is what the source-coverage test compares. The observation after a title is the part of the study bullet that shows the practice, with no bold inside it and no first person. The guide's `Applying an observation` bullets are four: compose for the evidence, visual choices that reinforce the action, review with the promised access needs, and sizes that follow purpose.
test/frontend-first-screen.test.mjs fails when an entry titled for the phone, a narrow screen, mobile or one column does not describe the phone, when a chapter entry speaks in the first person or carries bold, when the counted list is missing or follows the chapters, or when a counted entry's number differs from the chapters it names or names one that does not exist.
The archive had 844 bytes of room after run c998570f (`npm pack --dry-run --json`, 1,362,304 of 1,363,148). The guide cannot grow without trimming elsewhere or the owner raising the budget.
Select the opening by the evidence the visitor needs. The photograph with the finder over it remains a strong dealership example. Other pages can start with comparable items, an inspectable example, or the working records. Set the applicable measurement scope before markup.
references/patterns.md groups patterns by the decision being made: first screen, navigation and header, records and cards, results and filters, trust and reassurance, plans and prices, words, phone. Each entry is one line that says when to use it and what to build.
Step 3 has the builder choose a look as five choices from the subject, each with a reason: canvas (white, warm tint, dark), type voice (six, in references/typography.md), corners, color source (six, in references/color.md) and one or two signature moves. The surface-specific observations and access requirements that follow are checked in the render.
references/color.md and references/typography.md present a warm canvas and a serif headline as choices. An earlier version ruled them out; one of the owner's models uses both.
The pair is not a free choice. Built from one-line briefs without the method, three of three sites set the page background to cream (#f5f0e6, #f6f3ee, #f4eee3) and two set the headline in Fraunces, while builders using the method guessed their own default was a dark page with gold; the card shop built with the method chose ivory #f4efe4 and Instrument Serif. Step 3 ("Move off the unguided look") states that measured default and forbids pairing a warm tint with a serif headline unless a brand the builder was given sets both; the sheet records "Against the unguided look", and generic tell 40 fails a page.
The probe returns `look`: `backgrounds`, up to eight [colour or "picture", share of the page's letters] pairs, and `headline` {family, size, text} or null. Each piece of text counts toward the first opaque surface on it or an ancestor. A `url()` background image is a picture; a gradient is a surface, the solid colour under it or else its opaque stops averaged, and one with no opaque stop is skipped as a glow. The headline is the font-family list with the most letters among first-screen text at 80 percent or more of the largest size, skipping text whose box is smaller than one line (a title clipped out of sight), so a larger handwritten accent word does not stand for the headline.
The judge groups the backgrounds into a warm tint (`warmTint`: light 0.75 or more, red ≥ green ≥ blue, red at least 4 above blue, which takes in color.md's #fbfaf7 and leaves out #f9f9f8 and #f4f4f1), white or a neutral light, dark or coloured, and pictures, and the group with the most text is the canvas. It fails a warm canvas under a headline `serifFamily` calls a serif: a first family containing "serif" and not "sans", one in its list of common serif families, or an unknown family whose list ends in the generic `serif`. `--brand` makes that a note. The look is judged once, on the desktop view when there is one; a result without `look` gets a note and judges as before. The closing line names the unguided look.
scripts/first-screen.mjs exports `probe` (a self-contained function to evaluate in a rendered page), `judge`, `judgeView` and `FLOOR`. `--probe` prints the probe as one expression; a result file is judged into pass, FAIL and note lines.
The probe also returns `access`, counted over the whole page: the page language and title, images with no alt attribute, fields with no label, links and buttons with no name, tap targets under 24 px with another target within 12 px of the centre, controls that look the same focused and unfocused, and text below contrast on a solid background.
The judge fails each of those and names up to three elements. It reports without a verdict pictures of 80 px or more with an empty alt, buttons and fields under 44 px tall, and controls whose focus it could not read.
Text on a photograph, a gradient or a see-through surface is measured by scripts/photo-text.mjs from two full-page PNG screenshots per view: one after `--mark-text` paints every letter #ff00ff, one after `--hide-text` makes every letter transparent. Both expressions change colour only, load lazy images and wait for each image's load event, and scroll to the top. The pixels that moved at least halfway toward magenta are the letters; the hidden screenshot holds what is behind them.
A script in the page cannot read those pixels itself: an image from another origin, or any image on a file:// page, taints a canvas. That is why the measurement needs screenshots.
The probe records up to 40 such pieces in `access.photoText`, each with its tell, colour, needed ratio and up to four line rectangles in page coordinates, clipped to every ancestor that clips overflow. It also returns `pixelRatio`, `pageHeight` and `textCovered`. All four are optional when judging, so earlier results still judge.
The rule is the method's, FLOOR.photoCover 0.9: the text's colour, blended by its alpha, meets 4.5:1, or 3:1 when large, against what is behind at least 90 percent of its letter pixels. WCAG gives no method for text on a photograph.
The judge reports without a verdict text outside the screenshots (a row that scrolls sideways), text whose letters do not show in them, text where more than a fifth of the pixels around the letters differ between the two shots (the page changed), text below the first screen when the full-page shot's height differs from the probe's `pageHeight` by more than 2 px or the two shots differ in height (the page moved), and text past the 40 recorded. It refuses with no verdict two shots of different widths, a width that is not the view's width times the pixel ratio (up to 24 px more for a scrollbar, and the page's sideways overflow more for a page that scrolls sideways), one shot without the other, and a file that is not an 8-bit, non-interlaced PNG.
Each recorded line is trimmed where another piece of text overlaps it from above or below, because with tight leading a line's box takes in the next line's letters, which the marked shot paints the same magenta; the measurement adds 2 px to each side of a line and none above or below.
Text in an SVG drawing is read by its fill and always measured from the screenshots. Text that lies under something drawn on top of it, found by hit-testing each line's centre with pointer events forced on and the scroll put back at once, is counted as `textCovered` and not judged. A colour the browser returns as oklch(), lab() or color() is converted through a one-pixel canvas. Text drawn in a transparent colour is not judged.
The access numbers are WCAG 2.2 AA (target size 2.5.8, contrast 1.4.3), not measurements of the studied sites. The probe runs in the page and cannot read FLOOR, so it carries its own copy in `limits`; the test fails when the two differ.
Target size follows the WCAG spacing exception: a target under 24 px passes when a 24 px circle on its centre reaches no other target. A link inside a sentence is exempt, a control clipped out of sight or parked off the page is skipped, as is one that is inert or takes no pointer events, and a target under a pinned (fixed or sticky) ancestor is compared only with targets under the same one, because a pinned bar slides over the page.
Focus is read by focusing each control from script, with transitions switched off, and comparing the outline, shadow, border, background and color of the control, its labels, its first twelve descendants and five ancestors before and after. A ring drawn on a wrapper with :focus-within, on a label with :has, or on a child element counts. The probe then removes its style and returns focus and scroll.
Contrast is measured against the first solid background on the text's element or an ancestor. Text that overlaps a picture or a positioned block with a fill, or sits on a background image, a positioned pseudo-element of 12 px or more, or a translucent or faded surface, goes to the screenshot measurement. Text clipped out of sight for a screen reader is skipped.
`--access` prints the access lines alone, for a page that is not the home page. Step 5 has the builder judge every other page that way.
A result with no `access` was saved by the earlier probe: it judges as before with a note that access was not measured. An `access` that is present and malformed gets no verdict. A byte-order mark before the JSON is ignored.
FLOOR is actions 12 (desktop first screen), phoneActions 6, destinations 3, screens 3, surfaceFactor 2, pictures 4, pictureShare 0.25, targetSize 24, contrast 4.5, largeContrast 3 and comfortTarget 44 (reported, not judged). A desktop first screen passes on pictures with four or more, or with at least one whose largest covers a quarter of the screen. The phone picture count is a note, because one studied phone page opens with none.
A first screen with surfaceFactor times its actions floor (24 on a desktop, 12 on a phone) is a working surface and is not failed for height. `--one-page` turns the destinations check into a note.
The judge needs `pictures` and `largestPictureShare` in a result. The probe has always returned both, so results saved before the picture floor still judge.
The judge's closing line names the failure: a brochure when actions, destinations or height fail, a bare first screen when pictures fail, and a page that shuts some visitors out when an access count fails.
The judge exits 0 with a verdict and 2 with no verdict for anything that is not a probe result; it never exits 1. A view narrower than 700 px is judged as a phone.
The probe reads the viewport from document.documentElement.clientWidth and clientHeight. Under phone emulation window.innerWidth grows to fit overflowing content, which hid the overflow and misread the view as a desktop.
The probe counts links, not pages: a link to a page not yet written counts as a destination, so the measurement that stands is the one taken when every page exists.
The floor numbers in SKILL.md's Step 4 table must equal FLOOR. test/frontend-first-screen.test.mjs fails when they differ, when a method file names a studied site, when a pattern entry lacks its use or build clause, when the sheet or the method loses one of the five look choices, when a model loses its measured row, when a link or heading anchor in the method does not resolve, when the method stops saying what the judge counts for access and what stays by hand, or when Step 3, tell 40, the sheet, color.md or typography.md stop naming the unguided look.
The suite opens no browser, so the probe's access measurements are tested only when CYC_TEST_PLAYWRIGHT names a Playwright package (CYC_TEST_BROWSER_CHANNEL a channel such as msedge): the test then opens a page with one planted defect of each kind, embedded in the test file, at both widths. A second opt-in test runs the probe, both expressions and both screenshots on a planted hero, where only white text on a pale photograph may fail. Both are skipped otherwise. The default suite checks the measurement on synthetic screenshots and the PNG reader on a 64 × 32 Edge screenshot, decoded to the same bytes PIL gives.
references/generic-tells.md numbers the brochure tells 23 to 28, the bare page 34 to 39 and the unchosen look 29 to 33 and 40; tells 1 to 22 keep their earlier numbers because craft-rules.md and visual-material.md cite them. Any of 21, 23 to 28, 34, 39 or 40 fails a page. Tell 39 is an opening that hides the material the visitor needs in order to choose. Tell 40 is the unguided look. No canvas, typeface or palette is a tell by itself; only that pair is.
scripts/contrast.mjs is unchanged; it takes two or eight opaque hex colors.
Run `node --test test/frontend-first-screen.test.mjs test/frontend-contrast.test.mjs`.
Limits: the probe counts elements, not usefulness or beauty, and does not see what covers an element. Two signed-out visits per site on one day, United States content. Target showed a human check on every visit, so its model is the least certain. Passing the floor is not evidence that a design is good.
Look limits: whether a face is a serif is read from its name, so a serif served under an alias or an unlisted name with no generic family is missed. Text laid over an `<img>` counts toward the surface of its container, not the photograph. The canvas is the group with the most text, so a cream page whose text sits mostly on white cards reads white. Undersold, one of the owner's eight models, is a cream page with a DM Serif Display headline and fails without `--brand`, which is the intended reading: a brand may set the pair, a builder choosing freely may not.
Access limits: the probe counts presence, not meaning. It does not know whether an alt describes the picture, whether the tab order makes sense, whether an error is announced, whether text stays readable on a video, a slideshow or an animation, or whether motion stops for a visitor who asked for less. Text on a photograph is measured only when the builder supplies both screenshots, and a soft text glow is not credited unless it lies behind the letters. It reads focus only where the browser shows keyboard focus after script focus, which headless Edge does. Clearing the access lines is not an audit.
Run on seven of the studied home pages on 2026-11-21, the probe threw nothing and reported at least one access FAIL on every page. Those reports were not checked by hand and are not recorded as defects of those sites. The studied sites are the bar for substance and look; the access numbers are stricter than they are.
That pass showed four false alarms no generated page had triggered, each fixed before the probe was recorded: a link clipped out of sight for a screen reader counted as a small target, a focus ring drawn on a child element read as no change, mock-up buttons that take no pointer events counted as targets, and text on a color block positioned behind one word measured against the page behind it.
With the access measurements first-screen.mjs is about 32 KB. Adding them put the installed engine 5,344 bytes and the packed archive 11,173 bytes over the 1.2 MiB release budget, and the owner raised it to 1.3 MiB (docs/context/context-docs.md). Measure both, with a fresh install and `npm pack --dry-run --json`, before starting a run that adds to the method.
The method is instructions for a model; no test establishes that a builder follows it or that the result looks good. Judge a change to it by building from a plain brief with and without it, and treat the owner's eye on the rendered pages as the verdict.
