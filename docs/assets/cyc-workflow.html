Supporting step explanations and evidence references

The basis for a trust claim

What can the team actually inspect?

Machine checks validate specific records and conditions. Agents supply investigation and review judgments. A record makes a claim inspectable; it does not make the claim independently true.

Acceptance criteria
Recorded / checkedCriterion IDs remain represented through handoffs. Passing Verify and Deliver require passing results with evidence.
Judgment still neededDo the criteria express the actual requirement, and does the evidence support each result?
Commands & candidate files
Recorded / checkedReceipts retain command arguments, exit status, output and a covered source fingerprint. Passing receipts require stable covered files.
Judgment still neededDid the checks exercise the intended behavior? A file fingerprint does not freeze external services or prove coverage of omitted dependencies.
Consumers & review
Recorded / checkedAll Scout consumer IDs need passing check evidence. No open recorded findings; resolutions and dismissals need evidence.
Judgment still neededWere the right consumers found, and is the review sound? Self-review and peer review are not human approval.
Current delivery evidence
Recorded / checkedDelivery requires current verification where applicable, current semantic review, criteria evidence and valid referenced artifact hashes. Completion rechecks final conditions.
Judgment still neededDo the documented limits give the team enough information to accept and maintain this particular change?
Recovery & retained knowledge
Recorded / checkedRetry history, a shared failure budget, invalidated later handoffs, and learning/ledger dispositions remain inspectable. The shared review refreshes during recorded mutations.
Judgment still neededAre attempts reported honestly and findings accurate? Local records are editable and unreported activity is invisible to the controller.
Host delivery & understanding
Recorded / checkedWhen active, host hooks check applicable response rules and relevant session-run outcomes. Explanation responsibilities apply throughout.
Judgment still neededHook files do not prove live activation. Supplying explanations does not establish that the engineer understands them.

One requirement, two different return paths

“Export every matching order.”

Illustrative example, not a diagnosis of the CYC repository.

The export must include every matching order while preserving access restrictions. A multiple-page check shows that the export contains only the first page. The symptom alone does not tell us where the repair belongs.

Case A · The iteration stops early

The server query supplies the correct records, but the export loop terminates before the last page. The design is still usable.

Return to implementation. Repair termination, repeat the pagination and affected-consumer checks, and review the final candidate.

Case B · The browser never had all the records

The design exports browser rows, but the browser only receives one page. Formatting those rows differently cannot meet the requirement.

Return to Scout. Inspect the authoritative query and access boundary, revise the design, then rebuild and verify multiple pages and denied access.

“The browser only has one page, so formatting its rows cannot produce the requested export. We need the server's filtered query with the same access restriction, plus checks for multiple pages and denied access.”

Retain the failed browser-only attempt and its evidence in the ledger so later work can recognize and revalidate that constraint.

The explanation behind the diagram

Twelve responsibilities, in detail

Each step identifies the mechanism, why it matters, evidence to inspect, the condition that forces reconsideration, and what to explain to the engineer. These disclosures work with a keyboard and remain available without scripts.

The skill labels name existing CYC methods selected by the agent as needed, not automatic invocations or extra runtime stages. The linked examples follow one illustrative task: exporting every authorized filtered record across pages. They show when a method helps and what it passes forward; they are not results from a real export implementation. Engineering explanation belongs throughout the workflow; Teach is for a requested lesson.

01Define observable successScout

Skills at this step: Scout · Frame

When they help. Scout establishes the target. Frame helps when the request leaves success, scope or constraints unclear.

Export example. “Export filtered results” could mean the visible page or every matching record. Define success as all matching, authorized records across pages, with excluded records absent.

What carries forward. Observable criteria and explicit scope that Build and Verify can use.

Mechanism
Turn the request into observable outcomes, constraints, and behavior that must remain intact. For an export, a downloaded file is not enough: the records and access rules must be correct.
Why it matters
The implementation and checks need a stable target. Otherwise a plausible result can pass while missing the actual requirement.
Evidence to inspect
Acceptance criteria with stable IDs, scope, and explicit exclusions. The controller preserves the configured criterion IDs through handoffs; the engineer and agent judge whether they describe the right outcome.
Reconsider when
A consequential requirement is unclear or the criteria describe the wrong behavior. Resolve it explicitly; do not silently weaken the target to fit the implementation.

Explain to the engineerExplain what will count as success and the existing behavior the change must protect.

02Select route & riskScout

Skills at this step: Scout · Challenge

When they help. Scout chooses the playbook and review depth. Select a focused Challenge pass when the plan crosses a consequential boundary.

Export example. An export that changes permission filtering needs deeper review than changing its button label. Flag authorization as a risk and plan checks for records the user cannot access.

What carries forward. The selected route, risk rationale and required review/checks; the plan is challenged at step 7.

Mechanism
Choose a route appropriate to implementation, investigation, teaching, or another task. For this common implementation route, identify consequential permissions, stored-data changes, and interface contracts.
Why it matters
Different work needs different evidence. A local copy edit and a shared authorization change should not receive identical investigation or checks.
Evidence to inspect
The selected playbook and project verification commands. Configuration is captured and protected during the run. Higher-risk work gets a focused Challenge pass on the plan; ordinary work continues without that extra pass.
Reconsider when
The chosen route cannot establish the requested outcome, or verification configuration needs a legitimate correction. Preserve the old outcome and explain any new run.

Explain to the engineerConnect the review depth and chosen checks to the actual behavior at risk.

03Retrieve matched historyScout

Skills at this step: Context · Error Ledger

When they help. Context retrieves history matched to touched paths. Error Ledger searches prior symptoms, attempted fixes and confirmed causes.

Export example. Read the export component notes and an earlier “CSV contains only the first page” incident. Carry forward why exporting the UI’s loaded rows failed.

What carries forward. Relevant decisions and failure evidence, or an explicit statement that no matching history exists.

Mechanism
Read the relevant component context, capability index if present, and selected error ledger. Read the matched documents, not only their titles or index entries.
Why it matters
A previous constraint can explain an unusual interface. A recorded failed approach can eliminate an otherwise attractive but already disproven option.
Evidence to inspect
References to matched history, the constraint or failed attempt that matters, or an honest explanation that no relevant history was found. Referenced assessment files are fingerprinted.
Reconsider when
History conflicts with the code, lacks supporting evidence, or describes a different version. Treat it as a lead to investigate rather than an established fact.

Explain to the engineerName the historical decision that affects the current design and why it still matters.

04Revalidate that historyScout

Skills at this step: Context · Understand

When they help. Context checks whether retained claims still describe the current files. Understand traces current behavior when the history conflicts with the implementation.

Export example. The notes describe client-side pagination, but the endpoint now paginates on the server. Trace the current request and query before reusing the old fix.

What carries forward. A freshness assessment and evidence-backed current behavior; stale history is a lead, not a premise.

Mechanism
Check the relevant code, revision history, and uncommitted changes against the claims in the context documents. Distinguish an unchanged constraint from an obsolete explanation.
Why it matters
A readable document can still describe an old system. Building on it without checking can reproduce a failure that current code no longer behaves like.
Evidence to inspect
Freshness reasoning tied to the inspected components and working state. The controller requires the record; the agent performs and interprets the comparison.
Reconsider when
A false premise or changed dependency invalidates the design. Update the relevant reasoning and trace its effect through the impact map.

Explain to the engineerExplain which old assumption was confirmed or replaced, and what that changes about the approach.

05Trace impact & choose designScout

Skills at this step: Impact · Design · Understand

When they help. Impact traces the affected data and consumers. Design shapes the change and its trade-offs; Understand supplies missing evidence about existing behavior.

Export example. Trace filter state → export endpoint → authorized query → CSV. Compare reusing the server query with exporting loaded UI rows, and identify any counts or other consumers sharing that query.

What carries forward. An impact map, the chosen design and a check for each affected behavior.

Mechanism
Trace the changed behavior through input, state, services, interfaces, persistence, and actual consumers. Choose reuse, extension, repair, or new construction with a reason.
Why it matters
A local fix can leave a count, export, or background reader inconsistent. Find those dependencies in the project rather than inventing a checklist of hypothetical components.
Evidence to inspect
Named consumers with stable IDs and behavior checks, affected paths, and a design rationale. An affected area is a verification concern, not automatic permission to expand the feature.
Reconsider when
A consumer is missed or the design relies on the wrong source of truth. Revisit the impact and design before adding patches around the symptom.

Explain to the engineerExplain why the change belongs at this boundary and how dependent behavior stays consistent.

06Explain & settle decisionsScout

Skills at this step: Scout · Teach

When they help. Scout explains the engineering choice and resolves consequential uncertainty. Teach adds a lesson or practice only when the engineer requests it.

Export example. Explain why UI rows represent one page while the server query represents all authorized matches. If the engineer asks how pagination works, Teach can walk through that mechanism.

What carries forward. The explanation and any resolved decision. Silence does not establish understanding or approval.

Mechanism
Explain the causal path and consequential trade-offs. Ask for input only when an unresolved answer changes the solution; investigate factual gaps where possible.
Why it matters
The engineer must be able to correct a mistaken assumption while it still affects the design. An unanswered consequential decision cannot be treated as agreement.
Evidence to inspect
Alignment recorded as clear or actually confirmed, with supporting context. A needs-input assessment cannot pass Scout. An explanation is not proof of comprehension or approval.
Reconsider when
A required decision or prerequisite is unavailable. Record a blocker rather than proceeding on invented confirmation.

Explain to the engineerExplain the engineering choice in the project's terms and identify the specific decision the engineer owns.

07Plan evaluable slicesScout

Skills at this step: Scout · Challenge

When they help. Scout plans evaluable slices. Challenge tests the consequential assumptions of a high-risk plan before implementation.

Export example. Separate query reuse, permission preservation and CSV output into checkable slices. Challenge whether the proposed export could bypass a filter or expose another user’s records.

What carries forward. A slice plan with checks and resolved risks, or a clearly recorded blocker.

Mechanism
Sequence implementation around behavior and dependencies. Identify the first useful slice, how it can fail, and how later slices build on it. Challenge consequential assumptions for high-risk work.
Why it matters
A list of files does not establish an implementation strategy. Small observable slices make it easier to associate a failure with the change that caused it.
Evidence to inspect
A proportional plan, preserved behavior, consumer checks, and risk-specific review. Plan quality and the fast route's Challenge pass are method responsibilities, not separately enforced runtime stages.
Reconsider when
A slice cannot be evaluated or a challenge defeats a design assumption. Revise the plan before using it as the basis for implementation.

Explain to the engineerExplain the dependency that determines the sequence and why each check is useful.

08Build in tested slicesBuild

Skills at this step: Build · Error Ledger

When they help. Build implements and checks one meaningful slice. Error Ledger records failed attempts immediately so recovery can use the actual evidence.

Export example. Reproduce a multi-page export that loses rows, then change the retrieval path. If a paging fix duplicates records, log its hypothesis, change and failing result before the next attempt.

What carries forward. A scoped change and slice evidence; failed attempts retain their signatures and outcomes.

Mechanism
Reproduce a bug, pin behavior before a refactor, or exercise a feature's intended outcome. Confirm that a new regression check can detect the defect, implement the slice, and inspect the diff and affected consumers.
Why it matters
A check that never exercised the defect can pass with the defect still present. Focused checks distinguish a working slice from a plausible-looking edit.
Evidence to inspect
Focused check results, changed artifacts, diff review, and attempted fixes recorded in the selected ledger. Existing checks or a justified skip can cover work with no new testable behavior.
Reconsider when
A focused check fails: diagnose before retrying. Repair locally when the design remains valid; revisit Scout when a dependency or premise has changed.

Explain to the engineerExplain the changed state or interface, why the repair belongs there, and what the focused result establishes.

09Retain useful project knowledgeBuild / Verify preparation

Skills at this step: Context · Error Ledger

When they help. Context retains useful verified facts with evidence and limits. Mark a decision adopted only with actual acceptance; keep undecided proposals pending. Error Ledger retains failure history.

Export example. Record the observed permission and pagination constraints, the failed UI-row approach, covered paths and evidence. Identify an adopted query decision only when acceptance is established. Update the index when useful knowledge was retained.

What carries forward. Useful component knowledge and its index entry, pending proposals, or no reusable learning with a reason. Finalize authorized context and ledger edits before final verification.

Mechanism
Create or update docs/context/<area>.md for each affected component or area with reusable knowledge. Append a dated entry explaining decisions and rejected alternatives, failed approaches, non-obvious dependencies or gotchas, checks, and open issues. Record covered file paths and the reviewed revision; for uncommitted work, include the base commit plus covered-file evidence. Update docs/context/INDEX.md so a future task can find the entry by affected path. Supersede outdated entries explicitly rather than silently rewriting their history. Finalize authorized context/index and ledger edits before final verification. Retain verified technical facts with evidence and limits; keep undecided proposals pending and mark decisions adopted only with actual acceptance.
Why it matters
The next engineer or agent can recover why this component works this way and which approaches already failed, instead of repeating the investigation. The document belongs to the component, not the chat session. Durable documentation is also part of the verified candidate: writing it afterward changes the covered files and invalidates earlier evidence.
Evidence to inspect
The component-document path, its index row, dated reasoning, covered paths, reviewed revision, and supporting check or error-ledger references. Record the learning disposition as recorded, pending acceptance, or no reusable learning with a reason; do not create filler documentation. Bug-fix runs and runs with recorded failures also require selected-ledger evidence for delivery.
Reconsider when
A covered file still needs changing after checks, or a pending claim lacks acceptance. Re-verify changed files; distinguish verified facts, undecided proposals and actually adopted decisions.

Explain to the engineerShow the component document and its index entry. Explain which decision, failed approach, or dependency it preserves and how a future change will retrieve and revalidate that knowledge.

10Prove tests & verify behaviorVerify

Skills at this step: Verify

When they help. Verify proves applicable changed tests against the original code, runs configured checks and inspects the relevant user journey. A recorded exemption states its actual limit.

Export example. Show that the new multi-page export regression fails on the original bug, then passes on the repaired code. Check membership, duplicates, unauthorized records and the CSV consumer.

What carries forward. Original-code proof where required, configured check receipts and consumer evidence on current covered files, with exemptions and limits disclosed.

Mechanism
Prove applicable changed tests against the original code, then run journey or recipe checks and every configured command. Feature and bug-fix tests must fail on the original code; refactor tests must pass there. Record exemptions with their actual reason and limits. Map results to acceptance criteria and consumers; include rejected cases where they protect a requirement.
Why it matters
A passing unit test may not establish the real journey. Tests also lose relevance when the candidate changes after they run.
Evidence to inspect
Command arguments, exit status, bounded output, source fingerprints, criterion evidence, and named consumer results. Passing receipts require all configured commands to pass and covered source to remain stable.
Reconsider when
A check fails, targets the wrong build or data, or covered files change. Diagnose the observation; repair or correct an unprotected check with a stated reason and obtain fresh evidence. A legitimate protected-check, configuration, or criterion change needs a new authorized run while retaining the old outcome. A guard-detected protected or out-of-scope edit halts the run and preserves the files for review; a halted run cannot resume.

Explain to the engineerExplain the path each consequential check exercises, the defect it could detect, and what remains unverified.

11Review intent & code qualityVerify

Skills at this step: Verify · Challenge

When they help. Verify assesses intent, consumers and conventions. A focused Challenge pass can test a consequential claim or requested review beyond a green check.

Export example. A download test passes, but it only checks that a file exists. Challenge the claim that this proves complete, authorized results; inspect the query and ask for membership evidence.

What carries forward. Review findings and their resolution evidence, or a diagnosis that routes work back to the affected responsibility.

Mechanism
Review the current diff for intent, affected consumers, and conventions. Resolve findings or dismiss them with evidence. Label self-review and peer review accurately.
Why it matters
A CSV can download successfully while including only the visible page. Green commands cannot compensate for tests that encode an incomplete requirement.
Evidence to inspect
No open recorded findings; resolution or dismissal evidence is required. Every Scout consumer needs a passing recorded check. The controller validates these records, not the truth of the judgment.
Reconsider when
A finding exposes wrong behavior, a missed consumer, or an invalid premise. Route to the work that can repair the cause. Changes after this handoff require fresh verification and semantic review.

Explain to the engineerExplain the consequence of each material finding and why the evidence supports resolving or dismissing it.

12Account for outcome & learningDeliver

Skills at this step: Deliver · Context

When they help. Deliver accounts for criteria, evidence and limitations. Context supplies the durable or pending learning disposition.

Export example. Explain the change from visible-page rows to the authorized filtered query, show which checks ran, and point the engineer to the component notes and any pending finding.

What carries forward. A shared review artifact and honest handoff; completion alone does not establish human acceptance or permission to publish.

Mechanism
Record criterion outcomes, learning and ledger disposition, and material limits. Reuse the generated review, which refreshes during recorded mutations; request run completion only after the passing handoffs.
Why it matters
A persuasive summary cannot substitute for a verified candidate. The team needs to locate the checks and rationale behind each claim.
Evidence to inspect
Current executable evidence where required, a current semantic assessment, criterion results, and referenced artifact hashes. Completion checks final conditions; active host hooks separately check response readiness.
Reconsider when
Evidence is missing or stale: return to the affected work before completion. A completed run is terminal; later requested revisions require a new scoped run.

Explain to the engineerExplain the before/after mechanism, consequential trade-off, evidence locations, and maintenance implications.
